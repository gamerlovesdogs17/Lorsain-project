# Lorsain World Bible

## 1. Scope and tone

Lorsain is an Earth-scale modern political world, but it is not an alternate Earth. The technology level in 2028 is broadly contemporary: commercial aviation, container shipping, nuclear power, global digital communications, social media, precision-guided weapons, satellites, modern finance and mature welfare states coexist with large regional inequalities. The political system is intentionally recognizable enough for players to reason about it while remaining free of real-world country obligations.

The world population is roughly **3.4 billion**. There are **48 sovereign states** on the canonical v1 map. A few states are continental-scale powers, most are middle powers, and a significant number are small monarchies, island republics and border states. The central geographic fact is the **Meridian Sea**, which links the western Arden landmass, the Central Sea states and the Vesperan east. Terena sits on its western shore at the junction of continental and maritime trade.

## 2. Geographic regions

### North Arden
A cool-temperate northern belt of old industrial kingdoms and republics. It industrialized earliest and remains wealthy, urban and institution-heavy. Soland, Montria and Sholed form its commercial core. The region contains some of the world's oldest parliamentary traditions.

### West Arden
A broader and more unequal continental zone. The Soburish Federation is the largest power here. Sidbara, Kudren, Mazgara and Ugol have very different regimes but share overland trade routes and a history of border wars. The region is rich in minerals, grain and heavy industry.

### Meridian Basin
The states immediately surrounding Terena and the inland seas west of the Meridian. Vaskara is the dominant land power. Terena, Graeven, Nigis, Bharesh and Krigh form a dense belt of medium-sized states whose diplomacy is shaped by access to ports, pipelines, mountain passes and the fear of being subordinated by a larger neighbor.

### Central Sea
A chain of peninsulas, islands and bridge states. Siramel is the region's great power. Kraker, Srus, Kopjaj and the smaller island republics benefit from maritime trade and act as diplomatic intermediaries between Arden and Vespera.

### North Vespera
A densely populated northern arc containing Redcham, Gneaba, Thulta and Nash. It is economically integrated but politically divided between democratic, monarchic and centralized systems.

### East Vespera
A long eastern coast and interior containing Joim and the Dieric Federation alongside many smaller states. Maritime commerce is vital, as are disputes over fisheries, straits and offshore energy.

### South Vespera
The Elzesh Union and Capreshan Commonwealth dominate the southern continent. Elzesh is a centralized great power; Capresha is a looser parliamentary state and a major agricultural exporter.

## 3. Deep history

### Before 500 BCE: river societies and coastal exchange
Permanent agriculture developed independently around the main western river systems, the northern Vesperan basins and the warm southern coasts. Early states were local rather than continental. The Meridian Sea became the first true international system: port cities traded metals, salt, grain and ceramics long before rulers could project power far inland.

### 500 BCE–600 CE: the classical maritime age
Several successive merchant leagues controlled the Meridian straits. None conquered the entire sea. Their enduring legacy was legal: standardized weights, maritime insurance, written commercial contracts and the principle that foreign merchants could be protected by treaty. In Arden, larger inland kingdoms formed around cavalry and river taxation. In Vespera, bureaucratic monarchies developed earlier and maintained larger standing armies.

### 600–1350: fragmented kingdoms and religious commonwealths
The collapse of the classical trade leagues reduced long-distance commerce for several centuries. Religious institutions became the main cross-border networks. Monasteries, temple estates and urban guilds preserved education and finance. Many modern borders still follow the boundaries of medieval dioceses, crown lands or trade-city hinterlands.

### 1350–1650: gunpowder states and the opening of the oceans
Improved navigation and artillery favored centralized coastal monarchies. Montria, Soland, Siramel and several Vesperan states developed blue-water fleets. Overseas expansion was less about discovering empty continents than controlling islands, straits, ports and existing trade networks. The first permanent global embassies date from this period.

## 4. The industrial transformation, 1650–1860

Mechanized textile production began in North Arden and spread to Montria, the Soburish states and Vaskara. Railways and steam shipping transformed the Meridian Basin during the early nineteenth century. Industrialization produced a new political conflict that still structures Lorsain: property-owning liberalism versus organized labor, with agrarian and religious movements forming a third pole.

Between 1780 and 1850, constitutional revolutions replaced many absolute monarchies with parliaments, elected estates or written charters. Some monarchies survived by accepting responsible government. Others fell. Vaskara instead built a centralized administrative state capable of rapid industrial mobilization.

## 5. The National Era, 1860–1908

Railways made mass armies possible and nationalism turned dynastic disputes into popular causes. The **Seven Nations War (1864–1871)** began as a Vaskaran border dispute and expanded across the Meridian Basin. It ended without a permanent hegemon but established two ideas that became central to twentieth-century diplomacy: formal alliance systems and general mobilization.

The postwar decades were economically dynamic. Labor parties, mass conservative parties and universal male suffrage spread rapidly. Women gained voting rights first in several North Arden states and then across most democracies between 1895 and 1925. Colonial possessions and protectorates began demanding representation or independence.

## 6. First Continental War, 1909–1916

A naval confrontation in the Central Sea escalated after alliance mobilizations. The conflict spread across three regions within months. Vaskara and several continental partners fought a coalition led by Sobura, Montria and Siramel. Terena, then a constitutional monarchy, initially attempted armed neutrality but entered the war after merchant shipping and border positions were attacked.

The war did not produce total conquest. It produced exhaustion. By 1916, mutinies, food shortages and fiscal collapse forced a negotiated settlement. Several monarchies fell, empires lost peripheral territories and the first global **Council of Nations** was established. The settlement was politically fragile because it imposed debts and border revisions without creating a strong enforcement mechanism.

## 7. Crisis era, 1917–1938

The 1920s combined electoral expansion with economic volatility. The **Credit Panic of 1929** became a worldwide depression after bank failures froze international lending. Unemployment exceeded 20 percent in several industrial states. Communist, nationalist, clerical-authoritarian and anti-system populist movements all gained strength.

Vaskara emerged from the depression under a centralized "national restoration" government. Elzesh underwent a state-socialist revolution and rapidly industrialized under a one-party system. Several democracies introduced emergency constitutional provisions that later became pathways to authoritarian rule. Terena's own democratic collapse in the 1930s grew from this environment.

## 8. Second Continental War, 1938–1946

The second war began with Vaskaran-backed border revisions and an invasion of a smaller Meridian state. A wider conflict followed when alliance guarantees were activated. Elzesh entered later after its frontier was attacked; Siramel and Sobura became the principal maritime and industrial coalition powers.

The war was more destructive than the first because air power and mechanized armies allowed deep attacks on cities and industrial networks. Terena's authoritarian government initially aligned economically with Vaskara while avoiding full belligerency. In 1942, military defeats abroad, strikes and a constitutional resistance movement forced a change of government; Terena entered the anti-Vaskaran coalition the following year.

The war ended in 1946 after the collapse of the Vaskaran wartime regime and coordinated offensives on multiple fronts. No nuclear weapons were used in combat, although the first successful nuclear tests occurred in the final months of the war.

## 9. The Long Deterrence, 1947–1991

The wartime coalition fractured almost immediately. Sobura led a bloc of market democracies. Elzesh organized a state-socialist security and trade system. Vaskara, occupied and constitutionally reorganized after the war, initially remained outside both camps before gradually rebuilding its power.

The period is called the **Long Deterrence** because nuclear weapons made direct war among great powers extremely risky. Competition instead moved into intelligence, proxy conflicts, arms sales, space technology, trade access and ideological influence. Decolonization accelerated from the 1950s through the 1970s. The modern World Assembly replaced the weak Council of Nations in 1950.

Terena's current constitutional order was created during this period after a domestic emergency-powers crisis. It joined the Democratic Concord in 1974 but maintained a doctrine of parliamentary authorization for long military deployments.

## 10. The 1991 settlement and globalization

Elzesh's economic stagnation and political reform ended the bipolar order without a major-power war. Several security structures dissolved. Trade barriers fell sharply during the 1990s, container shipping expanded and capital markets integrated. Sobura enjoyed a brief period of unmatched influence, while Siramel became the world's largest trading state in several manufacturing categories.

The **Lorsain Trade Organization (LTO)** was created in 1995. Its dispute system reduced tariff wars but never eliminated industrial policy, sanctions or strategic export controls.

## 11. The 2008 financial crisis

A property and credit bubble centered in North Arden and Sobura spread through globally linked banks. Governments prevented a full depression but public anger over rescues, unemployment and austerity transformed party systems. New populist parties appeared on both left and right. Terena's People's Movement and the modern Regional League both grew out of this period, though from different political traditions.

## 12. The multipolar turn, 2014–2028

Vaskara gradually abandoned the restrained foreign policy of the postwar decades and began using energy exports, cyber operations and security partnerships to restore a sphere of influence in the Meridian Basin. Elzesh moved from ideological isolation toward state-capitalist great-power competition. Siramel became more strategically assertive as its economic weight increased.

A series of maritime confrontations from 2019 to 2024 produced the **Straits Security Crisis**. Shipping insurance costs rose, several states expanded naval forces and the Democratic Concord adopted new mutual cyber-defense commitments. Energy prices spiked again after Vaskaran supply restrictions in 2024.

By 2028, Lorsain is neither in a cold war nor at peace in the optimistic 1990s sense. The great powers trade with one another while simultaneously building sanctions regimes, military partnerships and strategic supply chains.

## 13. Global institutions in 2028

**World Assembly (WA):** universal diplomatic organization founded in 1950. Its Security Council can authorize peacekeeping and sanctions, but great-power vetoes frequently block action.

**Lorsain Trade Organization (LTO):** 43 members. Handles tariffs, market access and commercial disputes. Vaskara and Elzesh remain members despite frequent disputes.

**Democratic Concord (DC):** political-security alliance created in 1951. Article 6 treats a deliberate armed attack on one member as a matter for collective response, but each member follows its own constitutional war-powers procedure. Terena joined in 1974.

**Continental Sovereignty Compact (CSC):** looser security and economic grouping centered on Vaskara and Elzesh. It rejects outside democracy conditions and emphasizes regime sovereignty. It is not as integrated as the Democratic Concord.

**Non-Aligned Forum (NAF):** diplomatic caucus rather than military alliance. It includes democracies, monarchies and authoritarian states that resist permanent bloc membership.

## 14. Great-power balance

**Soburish Federation:** still the strongest all-around military and financial power. Its domestic politics are polarized, and allies increasingly doubt its willingness to bear unlimited costs abroad.

**Siramel Commonwealth:** largest manufacturing and maritime-trade power. Democratic and federal, but increasingly protective of strategic industries. It is inside the Democratic Concord politically while maintaining an independent defense doctrine.

**Elzesh Union:** centralized one-party great power with large conventional forces and a major nuclear arsenal. It has opened its economy without opening its political system to the same degree.

**Vaskaran Federation:** Terena's most consequential neighbor. Vaskara combines elections with tight executive control, state-connected industry, a powerful security establishment and a stated right to protect Vaskaran communities abroad.

## 16. Global issues active at game start

The 2028 simulation begins with several persistent pressures rather than one scripted crisis. Vaskaran military modernization and coercive energy diplomacy concern the Meridian states. Housing costs and post-2024 inflation are politically salient across wealthy democracies. Climate adaptation is increasingly a budget issue because coastal flooding and summer heat are worsening. Shipping and semiconductor supply chains are being reorganized around political trust. Migration from poorer and conflict-affected states remains contentious. Nuclear deterrence is stable at the strategic level but increasingly complicated by cyber capabilities, drones and precision conventional weapons.

These issues should create probabilities and strategic incentives, not fixed story rails. A given save may produce détente, a trade war, a constitutional crisis inside a great power, a localized war or decades of relative calm.

# Republic of Terena — Country Bible

## 1. At a glance

**Official name:** Republic of Terena  
**Demonym:** Terenan  
**Capital:** Valen  
**Population (2028):** 72,000,000  
**Administrative divisions:** 20 provinces + Federal District of Valen  
**National Assembly:** 420 seats, elected in 48 multi-member STV constituencies  
**President:** directly elected by national ranked-choice voting for a five-year term, maximum two terms  
**Default game start:** 1 January 2028  
**Incumbent president:** Mara Velic, Civic Reform Party, term-limited in 2028

Terena is a wealthy but unevenly developed industrial-maritime republic on the western shore of the Meridian Sea. Its geography is politically useful: a large western interior opens into a dense eastern coast, while the south is broken into peninsulas and islands. Five land neighbors surround it, including the much larger Vaskaran Federation to the west. No Terenan government can treat foreign policy as optional.

## 2. National identity

Terenan political culture is built around a contradiction. Citizens are proud of a strong national state created through centuries of political unification, but they are deeply suspicious of concentrated executive power. The phrase most associated with the modern constitution is **"two mandates, neither master"**: the president receives a direct national mandate to administer the state, while the Assembly receives a separate mandate to make and control the law.

The national flag, anthem and civic holidays date mostly from the democratic restoration rather than the old monarchy. The monarchy is viewed historically rather than as a live political project. Even most conservatives defend the republican constitution.

## 3. People, language and religion

About 78 percent of residents identify primarily as Terenan in census questions. The largest minorities are Vaskaran-origin communities in the western and northern border regions, Nigan communities in the south, and Graeven communities along the eastern border. Mixed identity is common in cities.

Terenan is the national administrative language. Vaskaran, Nigan and Graeven have protected regional status where local population thresholds are met. The constitution guarantees public-school language access without establishing ethnic electoral rolls.

Religion matters culturally more than institutionally. Roughly two-fifths of the population belongs to the historic Valenic Communion, about one-tenth to smaller Reformed churches, and a large plurality describes itself as nonreligious or only culturally religious. There is no established church and religious parties have been minor since the 1970s.

## 4. Physical and political geography

The map uses the exact Azgaar state-41 coastline, lakes and islands. The western third contains large low-density provinces and old industrial corridors. The center contains the Valen river-and-lake system. The eastern shore is heavily urbanized, with ports facing the Meridian Sea. The southern island belt has a distinct maritime and regionalist political culture.

## 5. Constitutional order

The current constitution was ratified in 1971 after the October Crisis exposed the dangers of the previous strong-presidency system.

### President

The president is both head of state and chief executive. The office is directly elected in a single nationwide ranked-choice election. A term lasts five years and no person may serve more than two elected terms. The president appoints ministers, executes laws, directs diplomacy, commands the armed forces subject to statutory war powers, nominates senior judges and can return legislation to the Assembly once.

The president **cannot dissolve the Assembly** and cannot call an early legislative election. Executive orders must be grounded in a statute or an enumerated constitutional power.

### National Assembly

The National Assembly has 420 members elected every four years by STV in 48 constituencies. District magnitude in the current map ranges from 7 to 10 seats and tracks population. The Assembly elects its Speaker but does not elect the president or a prime minister.

The Assembly controls taxation, appropriations, legislation, statutory delegations, investigations and treaty ratification. Committees may subpoena documents and compel ministerial testimony under judicially enforceable rules.

### Suspensive veto

The president may return a bill once with objections. If an absolute majority of the Assembly passes it again, it becomes law. There is no two-thirds ordinary veto override.

### Ministerial censure

Ministers enter office when appointed by the president. The Assembly does not confirm ordinary ministers. It may, however, remove an individual minister by a **55 percent censure vote**. The president then appoints the replacement. This lets the legislature check an executive team without turning the country into a parliamentary system.

### Budget continuity

The president submits a budget, but the Assembly may rewrite it. If a new budget is not enacted on time, the previous year's appropriations continue temporarily under inflation and statutory-index rules. Terena therefore does not have American-style government shutdowns.

### Regulation review

Major executive regulations are published to the Assembly. A majority may annul a regulation within 60 days if it concludes that the executive exceeded or misused delegated authority. The underlying statute remains in force.

### War powers

The president may respond immediately to an attack and may deploy forces for up to 30 days without new authorization. Continued offensive operations require an Assembly authorization. Intelligence operations and small evacuations operate under separate statutes and oversight rules.

### Emergency powers

A presidential emergency declaration expires after 14 days. The Assembly may extend it in 30-day increments and may terminate it at any time. The Constitutional Court receives expedited jurisdiction over emergency-power challenges.

### Recall and removal

Ordinary political disagreement cannot remove a president. Impeachment for serious constitutional or criminal misconduct requires a two-thirds Assembly vote and judgment by the Constitutional Court. Separately, three-fifths of the Assembly may refer a **national presidential recall** to voters; a majority vote removes the president and triggers a new presidential election.

### Constitutional Court

The Court has nine judges serving nonrenewable 12-year staggered terms. The president nominates; three-fifths of the Assembly must confirm. The Court reviews statutes, emergency actions, election disputes and conflicts between provincial and national authority.

## 6. Territorial government

Provinces have directly elected councils and governors. They control local transport, land use, primary and secondary education administration, public hospitals, policing frameworks and portions of taxation defined by national statute. The constitution guarantees provincial existence but permits boundary changes by national law plus consent of affected provincial voters.

Municipal councils and mayors form the main entry point for political careers. A player does not have to begin in national politics.

## 7. Electoral system

### Presidential election

All voters rank presidential candidates. Counting uses instant-runoff ranked-choice voting. Parties therefore care about second-choice acceptability, cross-endorsements and candidate relationships. A candidate can lead first preferences and still lose after transfers.

### Assembly election

The 48 STV constituencies elect 420 members. Voters rank individual candidates, including multiple candidates from the same party. This is intentional: intra-party caucuses compete at the ballot box as well as inside the Assembly.

Parties may nominate more than one candidate in a constituency. Candidate popularity, factional endorsements and local organization therefore affect which wing of a party wins seats even when total party support barely changes.

**Constituency boundaries are independent of province boundaries.** Terena's provinces are historical governments with very unequal populations, from small island provinces to Aurel and Southmark. The National Electoral Commission therefore draws population-balanced multi-member electoral regions without a requirement to stay inside one province. Many constituencies cross provincial lines. This avoids giving tiny provinces disproportionate Assembly representation while preserving provinces as meaningful governments for governors, services and local politics.

### Labour Party

Labour is the largest party and the historic vehicle of organized labor. Its **Social Democratic Caucus** favors a regulated market economy and expansive public services. The **Workers' Left** favors more public ownership, stronger wealth taxation and a less interventionist foreign policy. The **Workers' Bloc** is union-heavy and economically left but more culturally moderate. **Reform Labour** is younger, urban and pro-housing, pro-competition and strongly pro-Democratic Concord.

### National Union

The National Union is the main center-right party. The **One Nation Caucus** combines fiscal restraint with social insurance and national institutions. **Market Conservatives** favor lower taxes, deregulation and trade. **National Conservatives** emphasize immigration restriction, policing and military strength and compete with the People's Movement.

### Civic Reform Party

Civic Reform grew from the democratic constitutional movement. **Liberal Reform** is socially liberal, pro-electoral reform and internationalist. **Civic Moderates** emphasize competent administration, balanced budgets and cross-party bargaining. President Mara Velic belongs to the latter wing.

### Green Alliance

The Greens combine climate policy, civil liberties, transit, housing reform and social progressivism. They are strongest among younger urban voters and in Solenne.

### Regional League

The League is a federation of provincial and agrarian organizations. It supports decentralization, farm cooperatives, fisheries, local infrastructure and stronger provincial fiscal autonomy. It is not a separatist party.

### People's Movement

The Movement combines protectionism, anti-elite rhetoric, stricter migration policy, skepticism of major institutions and opposition to parts of the Democratic Concord agenda. Its voters include former industrial Labour voters and former National Union voters.

## 9. Party nominations

Nomination rules are intentionally different by party. Labour uses a ranked member ballot with union-affiliated delegates contributing a minority share to the presidential nomination. National Union uses a closed member RCV after a parliamentary-caucus endorsement threshold. Civic Reform uses an open registered-supporter RCV. The Greens use a member convention with transferable votes. Regional League uses weighted provincial delegates. People's Movement uses a direct member ballot but requires candidates to win endorsements from several provincial organizations.

These rules should be represented mechanically rather than reduced to one generic primary system.

## 10. Full political history of Terena

### 900–1228: basin principalities
The territory of modern Terena was divided among river principalities, coastal trading cities and southern island lordships. Valen began as a fortified market at the junction of river routes rather than as the largest settlement. Maritime cities in modern Shorren and Solenne were wealthier for long periods.

### 1228: League of Valen
Six inland and coastal rulers formed the League of Valen for mutual defense and common toll rules. The League created a permanent council and common commercial court. Later constitutional lawyers would romanticize it as the first Terenan representative institution, although ordinary people had no political rights.

### 1452: Crown of Terena
A dynastic settlement united most of the northern and central territories under the Crown of Terena. Southern provinces remained semi-autonomous. The monarchy standardized currency and law but left provincial estates intact.

### 1580–1690: maritime expansion
Eastern ports expanded dramatically as Meridian commerce grew. The crown built a navy, established fortified trading stations abroad and fought repeated short wars over straits and customs rights. Merchant wealth made the eastern cities politically distinct from the agrarian interior.

### 1711: Act of Union
A succession crisis ended with the Act of Union, which incorporated the southern provinces and created a national treasury. Provincial elites accepted centralization in exchange for guaranteed estates and tax bargaining. The modern provincial map still reflects several of these old jurisdictions.

### 1784–1787: Charter Revolt
Tax increases imposed without provincial consultation triggered coordinated refusal by merchants, guilds and several provincial estates. The monarchy survived but accepted the **Charter of Valen**, creating an elected lower chamber with limited property suffrage. The principle that taxation required representation became part of national political culture.

### 1820–1860: industrial revolution
Railways linked the Darsen and Aurel industrial belts to the eastern ports. Coal, steel, machinery and shipbuilding transformed the economy. Working-class mutual-aid societies became legal in 1839 and unions in 1857. Mass urbanization created lasting regional differences between industrial western cities, commercial eastern ports and rural Southmark.

### 1868: constitutional settlement
After demonstrations and a military refusal to fire on crowds, the crown accepted responsible government. The monarch retained appointment and foreign-policy powers, but elected ministries became normal. The franchise expanded in 1872 and again in 1891.

### 1896: universal adult suffrage movement
A coalition of labor, liberal and provincial reform organizations won universal male suffrage and a large expansion of women's municipal voting rights. Full equal national suffrage followed in 1919.

### 1909–1916: First Continental War
Terena entered the war in 1911 after attacks on shipping and a Vaskaran border incursion. Casualties, rationing and inflation destroyed confidence in the monarchy and traditional parties. Veterans' organizations and unions demanded a written republican constitution.

### 1917: First Republic
King Marek IV abdicated rather than order the army against mass demonstrations. A constituent assembly established the First Republic with a powerful elected president and a weak bicameral legislature. The constitution was democratic but dangerously dependent on informal restraint by the presidency.

### 1920s: mass-party democracy
Labour, Liberal Republican and Conservative parties developed nationwide organizations. Public pensions and workplace insurance expanded. The country also accumulated foreign debt and speculative bank exposure.

### 1929–1933: depression and constitutional collapse
The global Credit Panic caused bank failures and industrial unemployment. Coalition governments collapsed repeatedly. President Adrian Varek used emergency articles intended for wartime finance to govern around the legislature. Courts initially tolerated the measures as temporary.

### 1934–1942: National Directorate
Varek's supporters transformed emergency government into the authoritarian National Directorate. Opposition newspapers were restricted, unions were placed under state supervision and presidential decrees replaced ordinary law in many areas. Elections continued locally but national competition was hollowed out. This period is the single most important negative reference point in modern Terenan constitutional debate.

### 1938–1943: war and resistance
The Directorate aligned economically with Vaskara at the beginning of the Second Continental War. It did not initially enter the war. As Vaskaran forces expanded and Terenan soldiers were deployed to "secure" border zones, strikes and underground opposition grew. A failed 1941 crackdown split the officer corps. In 1942 a coalition of legislators, judges, union leaders and military commanders forced Varek from office. Terena formally joined the anti-Vaskaran coalition in 1943.

### 1946: Second Republic
A postwar constitution restored elections but retained a strong presidency because wartime leaders wanted a decisive executive. The legislature was made unicameral, however, because the prewar upper chamber was blamed for elite obstruction. The new National Assembly had broader social representation and proportional elections.

### 1948–1965: reconstruction and social compact
Labour-led and centrist governments created universal health insurance, expanded pensions, built public housing and nationalized the rail grid. Conservative governments later preserved most of the settlement while encouraging private industry. Union density peaked above 40 percent.

### 1966–1970: October Crisis
A sequence of bombings, separatist violence in a small border movement and Vaskaran military pressure led President Karel Dravic to declare a national emergency. Dravic extended emergency orders repeatedly, censored some publications and attempted to bypass Assembly appropriations. In October 1969 police violence against a mass protest produced a national strike. The Constitutional Court ruled parts of the emergency program unlawful, but its authority under the 1946 constitution was ambiguous.

Dravic resigned in 1970 after losing military and party support. Unlike the 1930s collapse, the state remained democratic. Political leaders concluded that personal restraint was not enough and called a constitutional convention.

### 1971: Dual-Mandate Constitution
The convention created the present system. The president remained directly elected but lost dissolution power and received only a suspensive veto. The Assembly gained control over money, investigations, ministerial censure and statutory regulation. Emergency powers received automatic expiration. A stronger Constitutional Court was created. Presidential elections adopted national ranked-choice voting; Assembly elections adopted STV.

### 1974: first elections under the new order
The first full election produced divided government. Rather than causing collapse, the institutions forced bargaining. This experience gave the constitution legitimacy. Terena joined the Democratic Concord the same year after an Assembly ratification vote.

### 1978–1988: Labor Compact era
Center-left governments strengthened collective bargaining, created sectoral wage councils and expanded technical education. Productivity grew rapidly, but older heavy-industry regions became dependent on protected firms.

### 1989–2000: market reform
A Civic-Conservative reform coalition reduced tariffs, privatized several competitive state enterprises and created an independent central bank. Labour later accepted most of the framework while preserving universal welfare programs. Eastern ports and Valen prospered; Caldria and parts of Aurel experienced industrial decline.

### 2001–2007: confident middle-power period
Terena participated in Concord peacekeeping, expanded university enrollment and became a major logistics and advanced-machinery exporter. Politics appeared increasingly centrist.

### 2008–2012: financial crisis and party fragmentation
A housing and bank shock pushed unemployment sharply higher. A bank rescue passed with Labour, Civic and National Union votes and became deeply unpopular. Anti-austerity movements strengthened Labour's left; regional fiscal disputes revived the Regional League; anti-establishment right-populists consolidated into the People's Movement.

### 2014–2019: Vaskaran pressure
Vaskara increased military exercises near the border, funded sympathetic media networks and used energy contracts as leverage. Terena responded by diversifying energy imports, expanding cyber defense and increasing defense spending. The issue split Labour and strengthened the pro-Concord wing of Civic Reform.

### 2018: Mara Velic elected president
Civic Reform candidate Mara Velic won after transfers from Green and moderate Labour voters. Her first term focused on ethics rules, infrastructure and Concord coordination.

### 2020–2022: pandemic and recovery
Emergency public-health rules became the first major stress test of the 1971 emergency provisions. The Assembly extended emergency authority in short increments, and the Court struck down two executive regulations while leaving the main health framework intact. The system's ability to fight intensely without collapsing increased public confidence in the constitution.

### 2023: Velic reelected
Velic defeated National Union candidate Tomas Kalen on the final RCV round. Labour's candidate finished third but Labour voters broke heavily toward Velic after Kalen made an electoral pact with the People's Movement in several provinces.

### 2024–2026: inflation, energy shock and divided government
Vaskaran energy restrictions and shipping disruption drove inflation. The government introduced subsidies and accelerated nuclear, renewable and grid investment. In the 2026 Assembly election, Labour became the largest party while Civic Reform lost seats. Labour, Civic Reform and Greens elected Daria Soren Speaker but did not form a permanent governing coalition because the president is independently elected.

### 2028: open presidential succession
Velic is term-limited. No party controls the Assembly. Labour leads on economic trust, National Union leads on crime and immigration, Civic Reform leads on institutional competence, and Green candidates have unusually strong second-choice support. Vaskaran relations remain tense. The default game therefore begins with a competitive national election and a legislature where issue-by-issue coalitions are normal.

## 11. Economy

Terena is an advanced mixed economy. Major sectors include machinery, shipbuilding, logistics, finance, pharmaceuticals, food processing, energy technology and software. The west and north contain more legacy manufacturing; the east contains finance and export services; the south remains more agricultural and maritime.

Unions represent about 27 percent of workers nationally and much more in transport, manufacturing and the public sector. Sectoral bargaining covers more workers than direct union membership. Public ownership remains significant in rail infrastructure, the grid and a minority of energy generation, while most consumer and manufacturing firms are private.

The 2028 policy arguments concern housing supply, energy costs, productivity, industrial subsidies, taxation of high incomes and wealth, the future of sectoral bargaining and whether strategic industries should receive protection from Vaskaran or Elzesh competition.

## 12. Media and civil society

The **Terenan Public Service (TPS)** is an independent public broadcaster funded by a multi-year appropriation formula. Major private outlets include the centrist *Valen Record*, center-left *Workers' Daily*, conservative *National Ledger*, market-liberal *Exchange*, green-leaning *Coast & City*, and populist digital network *Direct Terena*.

Important organizations include the Terenan Confederation of Labor, Federation of Manufacturers, National Farmers Cooperative, Small Business League, Civil Liberties Forum, Climate Action Network, Veterans Federation and several provincial associations. Organizations maintain endorsements, issue ratings, lobbying budgets and relationships with individual politicians.

## 13. Foreign relations

**Vaskaran Federation:** principal security competitor and largest difficult trading partner. Relations begin at -40. Border communities, energy and industrial supply chains prevent simple decoupling.

**Graeven Republic:** closest formal neighbor ally, +75. The two states share intelligence, infrastructure and a defense-planning framework.

**Kingdom of Nigis:** close southern security and maritime partner, +55. Fisheries and labor mobility still generate disputes.

**Republic of Bharesh:** generally friendly, +30, but agricultural trade and water management produce recurring arguments.

**Kingdom of Krigh:** pragmatic relationship, +20. Conservative governments in Krigh sometimes distance themselves from the Democratic Concord, but defense channels remain open.

**Soburish Federation:** primary extra-regional security partner, +65. Terena values the alliance but resists automatic alignment with every Soburish intervention.

## 14. Defense and security

Terena maintains a professional military with a small reserve system, a capable air force, a navy built around frigates, submarines and coastal defense, and a large cyber command. It does not possess nuclear weapons. Democratic Concord nuclear deterrence is extended through allied guarantees.

The military is institutionally wary of domestic politics because of its role in the 1942 and 1970 regime transitions. Active-duty officers are prohibited from party activity. Parliamentary committees have broad classified oversight powers.

## 15. Starting political environment and game hooks

The default start is deliberately unsettled without being an automatic crisis. The presidential succession is open. The Assembly must pass a 2028 budget. A major rail modernization bill is stalled between Labour, Civic Reform and Regional League negotiators. National Union is deciding how far to cooperate with the People's Movement. The Greens are debating whether to prioritize a carbon price or housing/transit legislation. Vaskara is conducting a large military exercise near the border. Several high-profile Constitutional Court terms will expire during the next presidential term.

None of these outcomes should be scripted. They establish incentives, relationships and pending decisions that the simulation can resolve differently in each save.

## 16. Canonical starting figures

The top 30 national figures are stored in `data/terena_starting_figures.json`. They are canonical at the 2028 start. The rest of the initial political class should be generated **once during development from a fixed content seed and checked into the repository**, not regenerated every new game. Future politicians entering after 2028 may be procedurally generated from the political and demographic conditions of the save.

# Game Design Specification

## 1. Core premise

This is a single-player persistent political-world simulator inspired by the breadth of multiplayer political simulations, but every person other than the player is an autonomous NPC. The player controls exactly one politician at a time. The simulation is not a party-management god game.

The world must be capable of producing plausible political history with zero player input. A required development benchmark is that the game can simulate fifty years while the player repeatedly chooses **End Turn / No Action** and still generate elections, careers, retirements, party changes, legislation, scandals, governments, court appointments and foreign events without deadlocking.

## 2. Time

Normal time advances in **one-month turns**. Election nights, debates, leadership contests, constitutional crises, wars and other major events may interrupt the monthly flow with dedicated screens, but they do not change the underlying calendar.

The default scenario begins **1 January 2028**. Presidential election day is in October 2028. The next regular Assembly election is in 2030.

## 3. Player career

The player creates or selects one politician. The player may begin as a municipal politician, provincial legislator/governor, Assembly candidate, Assembly member or selected advanced-scenario officeholder. Offices must not form a mandatory ladder.

Losing an election does not automatically end the game. An out-of-office politician can seek party work, media roles, advocacy positions, appointments or a later comeback. Retirement or death normally produces a career-summary ending. An optional **Continue World** button allows the player to select another existing politician and continue the same simulation, while the former character remains part of history.

## 4. Information philosophy

Neither the player nor NPCs should have omniscient access to simulation truth. Polls, whip counts, rumors, fundraising estimates, intelligence reports and relationship descriptions are observations with error. Staff quality and political relationships improve information quality.

The UI should prefer labels such as **Friendly**, **Tense**, **Likely Yes**, or a vote range over raw hidden values. Exact debug numbers can exist in developer mode.

## 5. Determinism

All simulation randomness must use a seeded deterministic RNG owned by the simulation engine. `Math.random()` must never be used in gameplay logic. Given the same scenario seed and the same player actions, the simulation must reproduce the same history.

Randomness supplies uncertainty and variation; it must not replace causal modeling. Elections are not coin flips. Leadership elections are not arbitrary dice rolls. Random noise should matter most when underlying incentives are close.

## 6. NPC model

Each active politician has persistent traits, goals and memories. Required dimensions include ideology, issue salience, ambition, competence, charisma, campaigning, fundraising, legislative skill, executive skill, integrity, ego, risk tolerance, sociability, party loyalty, faction loyalty, pragmatism, institutionalism and retirement inclination.

Relationships are directional. A may trust B more than B trusts A. Store at least **affinity** and **trust** separately. Major political events create durable memory records: endorsements, betrayals, appointments, attacks, leadership support, major negotiations, scandals and repeated voting alliances. Trivial interactions may decay.

NPCs form plans from their own information. They may miscalculate. A politician can launch a leadership challenge believing they have the votes and lose badly.

## 7. Active population

Target roughly **800–1,200 fully active political NPCs** in v1. This includes national politicians, important provincial politicians, ministers, judges, party officials, major mayors and high-salience candidates. Lower municipal politics and minor organizations can be represented with lighter-weight entities until they become relevant.

The initial 2028 roster is canonical. Generate the large roster once from a fixed development seed, review it, and commit it as content. New post-2028 entrants are procedural.

## 8. Party and caucus system

Parties are institutions with rules, staff, funds, ideology ranges, nomination procedures, leadership offices and recognized internal caucuses. Caucuses have memberships, chairs, endorsements, discipline levels and issue priorities.

A politician's vote should not be determined simply by party or caucus. A general decision utility can combine policy preference, caucus recommendation, party loyalty, constituency preference, personal relationship, career incentive, leadership pressure, donor/organization pressure, information uncertainty and a small stochastic term.

Parties may split, merge, rename or collapse when institutional conditions are met. These should be rare, path-dependent events rather than random flavor events.

## 9. Elections

### Voters
Use aggregated demographic/geographic voter blocs rather than simulating 72 million individual people. Each constituency has distributions for class, age, education, urbanization, religion/culture, unionization, sector, region and issue salience.

### Support model
Underlying support responds to party baseline, candidate quality, ideology fit, campaign effort, endorsements, spending, incumbency, economy, government approval, scandals, local issues and turnout. A small correlated polling/election error is added only after fundamentals.

### Polls
Pollsters draw noisy samples from underlying support and have persistent house effects and quality ratings. The player sees polls, not engine truth.

### Campaign AI
NPC candidates independently fundraise, advertise, travel, debate, seek endorsements, choose issues, attack, defend and drop out. Their strategy depends on resources, polling beliefs, risk tolerance and goals.

### RCV and STV
Implement real count logic. Presidential RCV must preserve ballots/transfers. Assembly STV must rank individual candidates, apply quota and transfer rules, and allow same-party candidates to compete. Assembly electoral regions are population-balanced national districts and are **not required to follow province boundaries**; the map data records each district’s plurality province and cross-province population shares.

## 10. Legislature

Bills are entities with sponsors, policy effects, text/summary metadata, committees, amendments, procedural state and vote history. NPCs introduce legislation based on ideology, issue salience, electoral incentives and party strategy.

Committees control hearings and reports. The Speaker controls floor scheduling under rules that limit permanent burial of presidential Priority Bills. Party and caucus leaders whip votes, but individual members decide.

Vote decisions should be inspectable after the fact through explanations such as "supported because policy alignment + constituency + caucus; relationship with sponsor had little effect." Do not expose the exact utility formula in normal play.

## 11. Executive

The president appoints ministers and directs agencies within statutory authority. Ministers have their own competence, ideology, relationships, ambitions and public profile. Assembly ministerial-censure votes can remove them.

Executive actions consume political and administrative capacity. A president cannot personally micromanage every department without tradeoffs.

## 12. Courts

The Constitutional Court has nine persistent NPC judges with ideology, legal philosophy, institutionalism and relationships. Cases arise from laws and executive acts, not from a purely random deck. The court can invalidate laws, regulations, emergency actions and election procedures.

Precedents should be stored as simplified doctrine flags/weights so prior cases influence later legal outcomes without requiring a natural-language legal model.

## 13. Economy

The economy should be politically deep but computationally lighter than a grand-strategy economic simulator. Track GDP, productivity, unemployment, inflation, wages, inequality, debt, interest rates, housing costs, energy prices and major sectors nationally and by province.

Policy changes should have lags, uncertainty and distributional effects. Do not implement a one-variable "GDP down = government loses" rule. Voter groups care about different outcomes.

## 14. Organizations

Unions, business groups, professional associations, farm groups, advocacy organizations and media outlets have memberships, resources, issue priorities, endorsements and relationships. They lobby, spend, mobilize and rate politicians.

Endorsements should carry different value depending on constituency, credibility and member alignment.

## 15. Media

Media outlets have audience, ideological reputation, factual reputation, geography and format. News coverage should be generated from structured events with templates and controlled text variation. Runtime LLM calls are not required and should not be a core dependency.

Investigative stories can uncover real hidden behavior. False allegations may occur as political events, but the simulation must distinguish allegation from truth internally.

## 16. Scandals and ethics

Scandals should come from actions, hidden traits, financial conflicts, investigations or credible discoveries. They should be uncommon enough to matter. Severity depends on evidence, media trust, partisan context, prior reputation and whether the politician lies or cooperates.

## 17. Foreign affairs

All 48 states exist as persistent entities. Foreign governments have leadership, regime type, economy, military capability, relations, treaties, strategic goals and domestic constraints. Terena's president has meaningful diplomatic authority, while major treaties and prolonged war require Assembly involvement.

Foreign AI should be utility/rule based. It must be possible to simulate trade disputes, sanctions, military crises, alliances, elections in democratic foreign states and leadership changes in authoritarian ones without scripting every event.

## 18. History archive

Every save maintains an in-game historical database: elections, officeholders, cabinets, party leaders, caucus leaders, major bills, court appointments, landmark rulings, wars, treaties, party splits, scandals and major economic events.

A politician page should remain accessible after retirement or death. Long saves should feel like generated political history, not disposable runs.

## 19. Turn resolution order

Recommended monthly resolution sequence:

1. lock player actions and queued commitments
2. update information/polls/economic observations
3. NPC planning and candidacy decisions
4. party/caucus actions and negotiations
5. campaign actions
6. legislative/committee/executive actions
7. judicial processes due this month
8. organization/media actions
9. foreign-policy and international events
10. economy/demographic updates
11. elections, appointments, retirements and deaths due on date
12. relationship/memory updates and decay
13. history log and news generation
14. save checkpoint and UI digest

## 20. Non-negotiable simulation rules

The engine never gives the player hidden bonuses because they are human. NPCs use the same core rules as the player. The presidency is not the only meaningful objective. The game must support failure and recovery. Political change must be path-dependent. The simulation should prefer causal systems over arbitrary event cards, while still using events for shocks that genuinely originate outside normal political behavior.

# Full Implementation Plan

## 1. Recommended product shape

Build the game first as a **desktop-class web application**: React + TypeScript UI with a pure TypeScript simulation engine. Keep the simulation independent of React so it can later run in a Web Worker, Node test harness, server process or Tauri desktop wrapper without rewriting game rules.

Recommended stack:

- TypeScript 5+
- React 19 + Vite
- pure TypeScript simulation package
- Zod for content/save validation
- Dexie/IndexedDB for local save persistence
- Web Worker for turn simulation
- Zustand or equivalent for UI-only state; simulation state remains engine-owned
- SVG + lightweight D3 helpers for maps and charts
- Vitest for unit/property tests
- Playwright for end-to-end flows
- optional Tauri packaging after browser vertical slice is stable

Avoid requiring a backend for the initial single-player release. Save files should be exportable/importable JSON or compressed binary bundles.

## 2. Repository layout

```text
/apps/game/                 React UI
/packages/sim/              deterministic simulation engine
/packages/content-schema/   Zod schemas + content types
/packages/map/              SVG/GeoJSON helpers
/packages/testing/          simulation harnesses, fixtures, metrics
/content/world/             countries, treaties, macro history
/content/terena/            constitution, parties, provinces, issues, orgs
/content/scenarios/2028/     canonical starting state and NPC roster
/public/maps/                world_political.svg, terena_game_map.svg (runtime geometry)
/scripts/                    content generation, validation, migrations
/docs/                       design/architecture documentation
```

## 3. Architectural boundary

The simulation engine receives immutable content definitions plus a mutable save state. It returns deterministic events/state changes. React never directly mutates simulation entities.

Conceptually:

```text
Static Content + Save State + Player Commands + RNG Seed
                        ↓
                   Simulation Engine
                        ↓
             New State + Event/News Log
                        ↓
                   UI View Models
```

This separation is the most important technical decision in the project.

## 4. Content layer

All canonical world data should be schema-validated on startup and in CI. Use stable string IDs, never array positions, as foreign keys. The Azgaar source IDs remain only in the crosswalk files and migration tools. `data/content_manifest.json` defines authoritative files. `terena_geography.json` is a derived convenience summary rather than a second independent source of truth.

Content categories include countries, provinces, constituencies, cities, issues, parties, factions, offices, ministries, committees, organizations, media outlets, historical politicians, treaties, election rules, constitutional rules and scenario events already in progress on 1 January 2028.

## 5. Deterministic RNG

Create one simulation RNG service, preferably xoshiro/xoroshiro with explicit serializable internal state. It supports named substreams such as `elections`, `npc-decisions`, `economy`, `health`, `foreign`, and `flavor`. Substreams prevent unrelated UI or flavor changes from changing election outcomes.

Every stochastic event written to the history log records enough metadata in debug builds to reproduce it.

## 6. Simulation state model

Use normalized entity dictionaries keyed by stable IDs. Do not keep large circular class graphs. Entity methods belong in systems; state entities remain serializable plain data.

Top-level save state should include calendar, RNG state, politicians, relationships, parties/factions, organizations, offices/officeholders, elections, campaigns, legislature, bills, executive, courts, economy, countries/relations, media, event queue, history index and player character.

## 7. Web Worker

Turn simulation should run in a Web Worker as soon as the core engine is stable. The UI posts a compact command bundle and receives progress events plus the final turn result. This prevents a 1,000-NPC monthly turn from freezing the interface.

The worker must be optional in tests; the same engine should run synchronously in Node/Vitest.

## 8. Save architecture

Each save has a `schemaVersion`, `contentVersion`, scenario ID and deterministic RNG state. Create migrations from day one. Never silently discard unknown or old fields.

Recommended persistence model:

- current normalized state snapshot
- compressed major-event history
- election result archive
- optional annual snapshots for fast historical browsing

Do not store a full duplicate world snapshot every month.

## 24. Performance targets

Avoid O(N²) all-politician relationship processing. Store sparse relationship edges and derive default neutrality. Recompute only relevant networks.

## 25. Narrative generation

Use structured templates, grammar variants and event metadata for news, messages and summaries. Runtime LLM use may be offered as an optional enhancement later, but the game must remain fully functional offline and deterministic without it.

## 26. Modding/data editing

Keep scenario content in human-readable JSON/JSON5 or YAML compiled into validated JSON. Provide stable schemas and an eventual dev-mode content inspector. Geography should remain SVG/GeoJSON. A mod should be able to replace parties, politicians and starting history without editing simulation source.

## 27. Major technical risks

**Simulation scope:** mitigate through vertical slices and abstraction levels.  
**NPC performance:** sparse relationships, event-driven updates and worker execution.  
**Unstable balance:** batch simulation and telemetry.  
**Save breakage:** schema versions and migrations from phase 0.  
**Map/data ID drift:** immutable canonical IDs and automated cross-reference tests.  
**AI feeling random:** explicit utility factors, memory, goals and imperfect knowledge.  
**UI overload:** progressive disclosure; show reasons and summaries before raw tables.

# Data Schema and Entity Contracts

## 1. ID policy

Use stable string IDs everywhere. SVG path IDs and content IDs are external contracts. Never use array index as the persisted identity of an entity.

Examples: `TER`, `W41`, `P09`, `FDV`, `C001`, `PARTY_LAB`, `NPC001`, `OFFICE_PRESIDENT`, `ORG_TCL`.

`W41` (world country), `TER` (domestic country) and `TERENA` (domestic SVG outline) are intentionally separate namespaces; use `canonical_crosswalk.json` rather than assuming they are interchangeable.

## 2. Static content versus save state

**Static content** defines geography, constitutional rules, issue definitions, party rules, initial politicians and historical facts before the scenario start. **Save state** records mutable values from the scenario onward. Do not modify static content objects during play.

# Testing, Simulation QA and Balance Plan

## 1. Determinism suite

Every CI run loads `TERENA_2028`, executes a fixed command script for 240 turns and compares a state hash. Save/reload at multiple points must produce the identical final hash. No gameplay system may call `Math.random()`.

## 2. Hands-off autonomy test

Run at least 100 saves for 600 monthly turns with a player who takes no political actions. Fail the build or balance candidate if a material share of runs show deadlocked election calendars, empty party leadership, permanent unfilled offices, impossible seat counts, runaway negative money, no legislation for years, constant civil war, or every party converging to identical ideology.

## 3. Election invariants

Presidential RCV tests must guarantee one valid winner, consistent transfer arithmetic and no creation/loss of non-exhausted ballots. STV tests must guarantee exactly 420 Assembly seats and exactly the constituency seat magnitude in each district.

Property tests should verify that materially increasing a candidate's support, holding everything else fixed, does not systematically lower their win probability.

## 4. Constituency sanity

Current `C001`–`C048` districts are population-balanced to approximately 1.17–1.67 million residents at the 72m baseline and elect 7–10 members. They are intentionally allowed to cross province boundaries. Automated tests should confirm geometry exists for every district, every Terena cell belongs to one constituency, all seat totals sum to 420, no constituency ID is duplicated, `plurality_province_id` matches the largest source-population share, and every `province_population_shares` vector sums to approximately 1.

## 5. NPC decision tests

Create small deterministic fixtures where motivations are obvious: loyal ally versus enemy, ideologically aligned bill versus opposed bill, safe incumbent versus suicidal challenge. The chosen action should be explainable from inputs. Then add uncertainty to verify that occasional mistakes occur only near reasonable margins.

## 6. Relationship tests

Endorsements, attacks, betrayals and appointments must alter directional affinity/trust as specified. Repeated small cooperative events should accumulate with caps. Memory decay must never invert a relationship by itself.

## 7. Legislative productivity

Across hands-off four-year Assembly terms, track bills introduced, committee survival, floor votes and enactments by policy area. Tune so hostile presidents and fragmented Assemblies reduce productivity but do not produce automatic paralysis.

## 8. Coalition diversity

Measure party pairings in successful votes. Economic, civil-liberties, defense, regional and agricultural bills should produce measurably different coalition patterns. If the exact same bloc votes together on nearly every issue, faction/constituency incentives are too weak.

## 9. Career mobility

Over 50-year simulations, inspect distributions of career paths. Some politicians should stay local, some remain long-serving Assembly members, some move into cabinet/governorships, a small number reach the presidency, and many lose or retire. A hardcoded universal ladder is a failure.

## 10. Scandal rate

Track public scandals per politician-year. They should be rare enough that a scandal is news, with most politicians never experiencing a major one. Investigations should more often produce no major finding than a career-ending revelation.

## 11. Economic calibration

Run policy-neutral baselines and shock scenarios. Economic series should remain bounded and exhibit realistic inertia. A tax or spending change should not transform GDP instantly. Provincial shocks should diffuse over time rather than teleport nationally in one turn.

## 12. Foreign-affairs calibration

In 100 fifty-year simulations, record wars, sanctions, alliance changes and crises. Desired behavior is a broad distribution: many saves have no great-power war, some have serious regional wars, and only a small minority escalate into system-wide conflict. Vaskara/Terena tension should raise risk without making war inevitable.

## 13. Performance benchmarks

Benchmark 1,000 active NPCs, 10,000 sparse relationship edges, a full Assembly, 48 constituencies and 48 foreign states. Monthly non-election turns should target <250 ms engine time on a mid-range desktop. Use profiling before adding micro-optimizations.

## 14. UI correctness

Playwright covers new game, character creation/selection, end turn, save, load, map interaction, election count, bill vote, relationship inspection and history pages. Tooltips must pull names/data from content rather than duplicate hardcoded strings.

## 15. Long-save migration

Maintain fixture saves from every released schema version. Migration tests upgrade them to current version and verify critical history, player identity, election results and officeholders remain intact.

## 16. Balance dashboards

Build developer-only reports for party vote share, seat share, incumbency, campaign spending, endorsement effects, bill passage, presidential approval, economic series, career outcomes and war frequency. Balance using batch distributions, not one memorable playthrough.
