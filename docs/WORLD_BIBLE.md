# Lorsain World Bible

## 1. Scope and tone

Lorsain is an Earth-scale modern political world, but it is not an alternate Earth. The technology level in 2028 is broadly contemporary: commercial aviation, container shipping, nuclear power, global digital communications, social media, precision-guided weapons, satellites, modern finance and mature welfare states coexist with large regional inequalities. The political system is intentionally recognizable enough for players to reason about it while remaining free of real-world country obligations.

The world population is roughly **3.4 billion**. There are **48 sovereign states** on the canonical v1 map. A few states are continental-scale powers, most are middle powers, and a significant number are small monarchies, island republics and border states. The central geographic fact is the **Meridian Sea**, which links the western Arden landmass, the Central Sea states and the Vesperan east. Terena sits on its western shore at the junction of continental and maritime trade.

## 2. Geographic regions

### North Arden
A cool-temperate northern belt of old industrial kingdoms and republics. It industrialized earliest and remains wealthy, urban and institution-heavy. Soland, Montria and Sholed form its commercial core. The region contains some of the world's oldest parliamentary traditions.

### West Arden
A broader and more unequal continental zone. The Soburish Federation is the largest power here. Sidburan, Kudren, Mazgara and Ugol have very different regimes but share overland trade routes and a history of border wars. The region is rich in minerals, grain and heavy industry.

### Meridian Basin
The states immediately surrounding Terena and the inland seas west of the Meridian. Vaskara is the dominant land power. Terena, Graeven, Nigis, Bharesh and Krigh form a dense belt of medium-sized states whose diplomacy is shaped by access to ports, pipelines, mountain passes and the fear of being subordinated by a larger neighbor.

### Central Sea
A chain of peninsulas, islands and bridge states. Siramel is the region's great power. Kraker, Srus, Kopjaj and the smaller island republics benefit from maritime trade and act as diplomatic intermediaries between Arden and Vespera.

### North Vespera
A densely populated northern arc containing Redcham, Gneaba, Thulta and Nash. It is economically integrated but politically divided between democratic, monarchic and centralized systems.

### East Vespera
A long eastern coast and interior containing Joim and the Dieric Federation alongside many smaller states. Maritime commerce is vital, as are disputes over fisheries, straits and offshore energy.

### South Vespera
The Elzesh Union and Capreshan Commonwealth dominate the southern continent. Elzesh is a centralized great power; Capresha is a looser parliamentary state and a major agricultural exporter.

## 3. Deep history

### Before 500 BCE: river societies and coastal exchange
Permanent agriculture developed independently around the main western river systems, the northern Vesperan basins and the warm southern coasts. Early states were local rather than continental. The Meridian Sea became the first true international system: port cities traded metals, salt, grain and ceramics long before rulers could project power far inland.

### 500 BCE–600 CE: the classical maritime age
Several successive merchant leagues controlled the Meridian straits. None conquered the entire sea. Their enduring legacy was legal: standardized weights, maritime insurance, written commercial contracts and the principle that foreign merchants could be protected by treaty. In Arden, larger inland kingdoms formed around cavalry and river taxation. In Vespera, bureaucratic monarchies developed earlier and maintained larger standing armies.

### 600–1350: fragmented kingdoms and religious commonwealths
The collapse of the classical trade leagues reduced long-distance commerce for several centuries. Religious institutions became the main cross-border networks. Monasteries, temple estates and urban guilds preserved education and finance. Many modern borders still follow the boundaries of medieval dioceses, crown lands or trade-city hinterlands.

### 1350–1650: gunpowder states and the opening of the oceans
Improved navigation and artillery favored centralized coastal monarchies. Montria, Soland, Siramel and several Vesperan states developed blue-water fleets. Overseas expansion was less about discovering empty continents than controlling islands, straits, ports and existing trade networks. The first permanent global embassies date from this period.

## 4. The industrial transformation, 1650–1860

Mechanized textile production began in North Arden and spread to Montria, the Soburish states and Vaskara. Railways and steam shipping transformed the Meridian Basin during the early nineteenth century. Industrialization produced a new political conflict that still structures Lorsain: property-owning liberalism versus organized labor, with agrarian and religious movements forming a third pole.

Between 1780 and 1850, constitutional revolutions replaced many absolute monarchies with parliaments, elected estates or written charters. Some monarchies survived by accepting responsible government. Others fell. Vaskara instead built a centralized administrative state capable of rapid industrial mobilization.

## 5. The National Era, 1860–1908

Railways made mass armies possible and nationalism turned dynastic disputes into popular causes. The **Seven Nations War (1864–1871)** began as a Vaskaran border dispute and expanded across the Meridian Basin. It ended without a permanent hegemon but established two ideas that became central to twentieth-century diplomacy: formal alliance systems and general mobilization.

The postwar decades were economically dynamic. Labor parties, mass conservative parties and universal male suffrage spread rapidly. Women gained voting rights first in several North Arden states and then across most democracies between 1895 and 1925. Colonial possessions and protectorates began demanding representation or independence.

## 6. First Continental War, 1909–1916

A naval confrontation in the Central Sea escalated after alliance mobilizations. The conflict spread across three regions within months. Vaskara and several continental partners fought a coalition led by Sobura, Montria and Siramel. Terena, then a constitutional monarchy, initially attempted armed neutrality but entered the war after merchant shipping and border positions were attacked.

The war did not produce total conquest. It produced exhaustion. By 1916, mutinies, food shortages and fiscal collapse forced a negotiated settlement. Several monarchies fell, empires lost peripheral territories and the first global **Council of Nations** was established. The settlement was politically fragile because it imposed debts and border revisions without creating a strong enforcement mechanism.

## 7. Crisis era, 1917–1938

The 1920s combined electoral expansion with economic volatility. The **Credit Panic of 1929** became a worldwide depression after bank failures froze international lending. Unemployment exceeded 20 percent in several industrial states. Communist, nationalist, clerical-authoritarian and anti-system populist movements all gained strength.

Vaskara emerged from the depression under a centralized "national restoration" government. Elzesh underwent a state-socialist revolution and rapidly industrialized under a one-party system. Several democracies introduced emergency constitutional provisions that later became pathways to authoritarian rule. Terena's own democratic collapse in the 1930s grew from this environment.

## 8. Second Continental War, 1938–1946

The second war began with Vaskaran-backed border revisions and an invasion of a smaller Meridian state. A wider conflict followed when alliance guarantees were activated. Elzesh entered later after its frontier was attacked; Siramel and Sobura became the principal maritime and industrial coalition powers.

The war was more destructive than the first because air power and mechanized armies allowed deep attacks on cities and industrial networks. Terena's authoritarian government initially aligned economically with Vaskara while avoiding full belligerency. In 1942, military defeats abroad, strikes and a constitutional resistance movement forced a change of government; Terena entered the anti-Vaskaran coalition the following year.

The war ended in 1946 after the collapse of the Vaskaran wartime regime and coordinated offensives on multiple fronts. No nuclear weapons were used in combat, although the first successful nuclear tests occurred in the final months of the war.

## 9. The Long Deterrence, 1947–1991

The wartime coalition fractured almost immediately. Sobura led a bloc of market democracies. Elzesh organized a state-socialist security and trade system. Vaskara, occupied and constitutionally reorganized after the war, initially remained outside both camps before gradually rebuilding its power.

The period is called the **Long Deterrence** because nuclear weapons made direct war among great powers extremely risky. Competition instead moved into intelligence, proxy conflicts, arms sales, space technology, trade access and ideological influence. Decolonization accelerated from the 1950s through the 1970s. The modern World Assembly replaced the weak Council of Nations in 1950.

Terena's current constitutional order was created during this period after a domestic emergency-powers crisis. It joined the Democratic Concord in 1974 but maintained a doctrine of parliamentary authorization for long military deployments.

## 10. The 1991 settlement and globalization

Elzesh's economic stagnation and political reform ended the bipolar order without a major-power war. Several security structures dissolved. Trade barriers fell sharply during the 1990s, container shipping expanded and capital markets integrated. Sobura enjoyed a brief period of unmatched influence, while Siramel became the world's largest trading state in several manufacturing categories.

The **Lorsain Trade Organization (LTO)** was created in 1995. Its dispute system reduced tariff wars but never eliminated industrial policy, sanctions or strategic export controls.

## 11. The 2008 financial crisis

A property and credit bubble centered in North Arden and Sobura spread through globally linked banks. Governments prevented a full depression but public anger over rescues, unemployment and austerity transformed party systems. New populist parties appeared on both left and right. Terena's People's Movement and the modern Regional League both grew out of this period, though from different political traditions.

## 12. The multipolar turn, 2014–2028

Vaskara gradually abandoned the restrained foreign policy of the postwar decades and began using energy exports, cyber operations and security partnerships to restore a sphere of influence in the Meridian Basin. Elzesh moved from ideological isolation toward state-capitalist great-power competition. Siramel became more strategically assertive as its economic weight increased.

A series of maritime confrontations from 2019 to 2024 produced the **Straits Security Crisis**. Shipping insurance costs rose, several states expanded naval forces and the Democratic Concord adopted new mutual cyber-defense commitments. Energy prices spiked again after Vaskaran supply restrictions in 2024.

By 2028, Lorsain is neither in a cold war nor at peace in the optimistic 1990s sense. The great powers trade with one another while simultaneously building sanctions regimes, military partnerships and strategic supply chains.

## 13. Global institutions in 2028

**World Assembly (WA):** universal diplomatic organization founded in 1950. Its Security Council can authorize peacekeeping and sanctions, but great-power vetoes frequently block action.

**Lorsain Trade Organization (LTO):** 43 members. Handles tariffs, market access and commercial disputes. Vaskara and Elzesh remain members despite frequent disputes.

**Democratic Concord (DC):** political-security alliance created in 1951. Article 6 treats a deliberate armed attack on one member as a matter for collective response, but each member follows its own constitutional war-powers procedure. Terena joined in 1974.

**Continental Sovereignty Compact (CSC):** looser security and economic grouping centered on Vaskara and Elzesh. It rejects outside democracy conditions and emphasizes regime sovereignty. It is not as integrated as the Democratic Concord.

**Non-Aligned Forum (NAF):** diplomatic caucus rather than military alliance. It includes democracies, monarchies and authoritarian states that resist permanent bloc membership.

## 14. Great-power balance

**Soburish Federation:** still the strongest all-around military and financial power. Its domestic politics are polarized, and allies increasingly doubt its willingness to bear unlimited costs abroad.

**Siramel Commonwealth:** largest manufacturing and maritime-trade power. Democratic and federal, but increasingly protective of strategic industries. It is inside the Democratic Concord politically while maintaining an independent defense doctrine.

**Elzesh Union:** centralized one-party great power with large conventional forces and a major nuclear arsenal. It has opened its economy without opening its political system to the same degree.

**Vaskaran Federation:** Terena's most consequential neighbor. Vaskara combines elections with tight executive control, state-connected industry, a powerful security establishment and a stated right to protect Vaskaran communities abroad.

## 16. Global issues active at game start

The 2028 simulation begins with several persistent pressures rather than one scripted crisis. Vaskaran military modernization and coercive energy diplomacy concern the Meridian states. Housing costs and post-2024 inflation are politically salient across wealthy democracies. Climate adaptation is increasingly a budget issue because coastal flooding and summer heat are worsening. Shipping and semiconductor supply chains are being reorganized around political trust. Migration from poorer and conflict-affected states remains contentious. Nuclear deterrence is stable at the strategic level but increasingly complicated by cyber capabilities, drones and precision conventional weapons.

These issues should create probabilities and strategic incentives, not fixed story rails. A given save may produce détente, a trade war, a constitutional crisis inside a great power, a localized war or decades of relative calm.
